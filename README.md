# Cyberpunk Tennis

Mobile-first tennis on an HTML canvas. Plain HTML and JavaScript, no build step:
open `index.html` through any static web server (or `_test.html` straight from disk,
which only adds the doctype and viewport tags the artifact host normally supplies).

## Files

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `index.html` | Markup, styles, menus, HUD |
| `js/physics.js` | Court geometry, ball flight (drag, topspin dip, sidespin curve, bounce), shot solver |
| `js/characters.js` | Octopus and Philosopher stats and their procedurally drawn sprites |
| `js/match.js` | Scoring: one set, 7-point tiebreak at 6-6 |
| `js/game.js` | Match engine: serving, hitting, timing, line calls, stamina, CPU |
| `js/achievements.js` | Achievements and stats: what counts, the per-match tracker, the record saved in localStorage |
| `js/render.js` | Perspective camera (chase or first person) and neon arena. A phone that can't keep up steps down a quality level (`watchFrameRate`): frames slower than ~40 fps while drawing one takes most of a 60 fps frame, or slower than ~22 fps. A steady 30 fps that draws quickly (an iPhone in Low Power Mode) keeps the full look |
| `js/vr.js` | Cardboard VR: head tracking from the motion sensor, and the one-button auto shots |
| `js/main.js` | Touch input, HUD, sound effects (realistic hits and bounces in zen mode), frame loop, tutorial lessons |
| `js/music.js` | Synthesised music: the chiptune match track, and zen mode's slow ambient track |
| `js/net.js`, `server/relay.js` | Online link: the Cloudflare Worker relay (one Durable Object per room code), its client, and the lag meter |
| `tests/` | Engine and relay tests (`npm test`) and the balance simulation (`npm run sim`) |

## How the spec maps to the code

- **Swipe = shot, tap = move.** `main.js` `handleSwipe` / `handleTap`.
- **Direction** is where the swipe points on screen: from the ball, along the swipe, to the back of the court (`pointAt` in `main.js`), so it allows for the camera's angle and perspective. The spot is kept 0.45 m inside the sidelines. **Swipe speed** sets `pace`: fast = fast and flat, slow = more spin and less speed (`computeShot`). A well-timed shot always lands in: when clearing the net would carry it long, it comes off with less pace. The exception is a flick faster than 7 screens a second, which overhits (`over`) and loses margin: from about 8, a full-pace drive or serve lands long and the shot callout says Overhit.
- **Up = topspin, down = backspin, a bowed swipe = sidespin** (`spinDir`, `curve`).
- **Lob:** a slow, long upward swipe (at least 0.22 screens, averaging under 1.4 screens per second over the whole swipe) sets `input.lob`, the same topspin lob the CPU uses. A player's lob always goes deep, its timing is judged from the middle of the swipe (`input.lead`, up to `LOB_LEAD`) rather than when the finger lifts, and its timing window is `LOB_EASE` times wider.
- **Timing.** Each wing has its own window per character (`window.fh` / `window.bh`). Being late or early by `err` drifts the shot: late forehand right, early forehand left, late backhand left, early backhand right. It also costs power and height in proportion to how far off it was (`powerF`), so a badly mistimed ball lands short or finds the net. Your position counts as extra mistiming (`balance` in `computeShot`): a ball jammed within 0.3 m of your body, one beyond 60% of your reach, or one hit while running faster than 30% of top speed. The shot callout says Jammed, Stretched or On the run. A swipe too early, too late or out of reach still swings the racket and misses (`swingMiss`); so does a CPU that gets to a ball a step too late. A swipe at a serve before it bounces counts as early, and a late swipe at a ball you could have reached says Too late, not Out of reach. The yellow X marks where a ball coming to you will bounce (`landingFor`); a serve shows it from the moment it is struck, since the ring only comes once it has bounced.
- **Chasing.** The CPU and automove (`cpuPlan`) run at a ball that will land up to `CHASE_OUT` (1 m) out, as a real player would before reading it as out, then let it go. A ball further out than that, they leave and recover.
- **Stamina.** Running drains it, and every metre run also lowers a hard cap for the rest of the set (`cap`). Back-to-back power groundstrokes cost 3, 6, 9…; a volley, power volley or overhead costs nothing extra and resets the count, so attacking the net isn't punished. Low stamina slows running, cuts power and narrows timing windows; below 35 the bar pulses red and the CPU starts running you side to side. Every swing costs 0.4. Between points you get back 30% of what you're missing (60% at a changeover), so a long rally leaves you tired for the next few points and stamina wears down over a set; waiting before you serve adds nothing.
- **Overheads** above the player's `overheadZ` are very fast and cost no stamina. **Power shots** happen above 1.3 m. **Volleys** happen when you meet the ball before it bounces.
- **Randomness** only appears in `cpuChooseShot` / `cpuChooseServe`. CPU errors on groundstrokes are deterministic: they come from being rushed, tired, the pace of the incoming ball, and how much pace the CPU itself chose. CPU serves get a random timing error, so some miss.
- **CPU tactics** come from each character's `tactics` block: Octopus plays steady pressure (deep, heavy balls with big margins, worked into your weaker wing, few errors, rarely goes for broke, passes or lobs a net rusher); Philosopher is aggressive and creative (big pace, goes for winners early, follows short balls in, serves and volleys, mixes in drop shots, short angles and hitting behind you, likes the wide serve). Each has its own point patterns (`patterns`), appetite for winners (`attack`) and error rate (`err`). The menu's level (Chill, Club, Pro) sets the ball speed and how the CPU plays (`CT.CPU_LEVELS` in `game.js`): how often it mistimes, how fast it reads a shot, how well it covers the angles, how fast it runs, how hard and deep it hits, how often it goes for winners (`attack`) and how much it thinks (`smart`). A thinking CPU plays a pattern each point (work the weak wing then the open court, side to side, or behind you), learns during the match which of your wings misses more and whether you like the net (`g.scout`), serves to a target (wide, body or T, second serves to your weaker wing), and takes more risk when behind and less when well ahead. Its rally ball goes cross-court from a wide position (`cross`). It mistimes both ways, early (pulled across) as well as late (pushed down the line), and its misses are split between long or wide, the net, and short sitters (`cpuHit`). The CPU keeps 0.4 m over the net and lifts low balls (a dug-out drop shot) instead of driving them.
- **Tutorial.** Eleven lessons against a ball machine (`drill` in `createGame`): serve, forehand, backhand, aim left and right, slice, drop shot, lob, then volley and smash at the net (`drill.feed`), and finally a real point against the Chill CPU. The machine (control `'feeder'`) never returns the ball; each rep ends at the first bounce with a `drill` event, and `LESSONS` in `main.js` decides which reps count.
- **Camera.** Chase (behind and above you, the default) or first person (from your eyes, with your racket in the corner). Switch with the 1P/3P button in the HUD, the Camera option on the menu, or the C key; the choice is remembered (`setView` in `render.js`).
- **Cardboard VR.** Pick VR under Camera. The first-person view is drawn twice, side by side, from two eyes 64 mm apart (`drawStereo` in `render.js`). The phone's motion sensor turns it (`screenPose` in `vr.js`), and the game never turns it for you. There's no HUD: the score goes on the billboard, as in zen mode. Automove always runs. In a headset you can't swipe, so there's one button: the headset's lever, an ordinary touch that stays down while held (the space bar and a gamepad's A work too). Let go as the ball arrives to hit, timed like the end of a swipe; a hold of 0.3 s or more makes it a power shot, flat and hard into the open court. Where the ball goes is picked for you (`autoShot` in `vr.js`): away from the opponent, a lob or a pass when they're at the net, now and then a drop shot when they're camped deep, and a serve wide, at the body or down the T. This random choice is the one place outside the CPU where the game rolls dice. Your racket is drawn in the world (`racketHead` in `render.js`), so each eye sees it in depth: it waits in front of you, takes a backswing as the ball comes, sweeps through the spot where the ball was struck and follows through, leaving a faint trail. It's worked out from the swing the game already plays. A small ring on the ball closes as it comes; an arrow points to a ball you can't see, and two ticks then a higher beep count down to the moment to let go (`timeToHit`). Press before play (and after a pause) to set straight ahead; press looking at your feet to pause, or looking up at the sky to recentre (a two-finger tap on the pad recentres at once). The pause menu is drawn in each eye, with Exit VR, Resume and Quit placed around you: turn to one and press. Exit VR goes back to the normal screen, still paused. A white line down the middle helps line the phone up with the headset's divider. For full swipe control, a second phone can be a **swipe pad**: the headset phone links it through the online relay with a room code of its own, the pad sends each swipe's points (as fractions of its screen's short side), and the headset phone plays them through the same swipe-to-shot code as the normal screen, taking half the measured round trip off the timing. A pad tap is the button. The menu's VR view, VR graphics and VR lenses settings fit the picture to the headset and the phone: Auto starts sharp and steps down when a frame takes too long, while Smooth, Balanced and Sharp fix the level. Each eye's scene is drawn into a picture slightly bigger than the eye that's moved and turned with your head every frame; when drawing both eyes takes over 9 ms, the scene is redrawn only every other frame while the picture still follows your head every frame (the timewarp VR headsets use). iPhones ask for motion access on the first tap; they can't go full screen, so add the game to the home screen.
- **Achievements and stats.** `createAchievementTracker` follows your side through a match from its `point` and `hit` events and says what each point earned; `main.js` saves it to localStorage (`ct.achievements`), puts each unlock on the billboard after the point's word (the common ones only the first time; an online guest sees the billboard over their own far court), and lists unlocked achievements and per-level stats on the Achievements screen. Locked ones aren't shown anywhere. Pro achievements only count against the Pro CPU; the tutorial doesn't count. A serve's speed (fastest serve, Rocket Serve) only counts once it has landed in.
- **Resume.** A match against the CPU is saved after every point (localStorage `ct.saved`: players, level, score, stamina, the CPU's scouting and the achievement tracker's progress; `saveMatch` in `main.js`, `CT.saveGameState` in `game.js`). Quit from the pause menu, or leave or reload the page, and the menu shows Resume match with the score; it carries on from the start of the next point. Finishing the set or starting a new match drops the save. Online matches and the tutorial aren't saved.
- **Serve clock.** Online, a server has 20 seconds to toss; running out costs the serve.
- **Lag.** Online, the phones ping each other every half second (`createLagMeter` in `net.js`). Over 150 ms the ping takes the tag's place on the billboard, yellow, and red over 400 ms. Over 450 ms the host stops the match and both phones say Paused, until the ping has been back under 450 ms for a second; taps and swipes do nothing meanwhile. `?lag=200` on the page URL holds each message back 200 ms, to try it.
- **Direct link.** Once both phones are in a room they try to connect straight to each other (a WebRTC data channel, set up through the room with Cloudflare's and Google's public STUN servers). When it opens, game messages skip the relay; the room stays connected as the fallback, and messages go back through it if the direct link never opens (some mobile networks block it) or drops mid-match. Nothing is lost or reordered at the switch: each phone's last message through the room tells the other to start reading the direct link. `?ping` on the page URL keeps the ping on the billboard, labelled DIRECT or RELAY; `?relay` never tries a direct link.
- **Rooms.** A room takes one phone a side. While both are connected, the relay turns away anyone else with the code, so nobody can knock the guest out of a match. A phone that dropped out can take its own place again: each phone keeps a random id (`ct.phoneId` in localStorage) that it sends to the relay.

## Tests

`npm test` runs the engine tests with Node's built-in test runner (Node 20+, nothing to install): scoring, the shot solver, timing penalties, whole CPU sets, the tutorial drills and online snapshots. GitHub Actions runs them on every push and pull request.

`npm run sim` plays seeded sets between a scripted player and each CPU level and prints who wins, how low stamina gets and how long the rallies are. Run it after changing anything that affects balance.

## Multiplayer later

All input goes through `CT.requestMove`, `CT.requestToss` and `CT.requestSwipe(game, side, input)`, and
`game.control` marks each side as `human` or `cpu`. An online mode can relay those calls between two
clients (or run the engine on a server) without changing the rules code; the simulation is fixed-step and deterministic.
